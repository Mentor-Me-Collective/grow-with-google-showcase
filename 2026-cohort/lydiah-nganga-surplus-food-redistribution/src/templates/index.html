Two ways surplus finds a home

Not every listing wants the same outcome.

Some surplus is right for a full donation to an organization already serving people in need. Some is better sold on, cheaply, to a neighbor who'd rather pay a little than see it thrown out. Vendors choose per listing.

Path A

Donate to a Pantry or NGO

  1. Vendor logs quantity, food type, and pickup window.
  2. Our engine scores nearby pantries and NGOs by distance, urgency, and capacity.
  3. The best match gets an automatic pickup alert.
  4. The pantry or NGO collects and redistributes through their existing network.
Path B

Sell affordable to the community

  1. Vendor lists surplus at a steep discount, good today, not tomorrow.
  2. The listing appears on the public community board.
  3. A neighbor reserves it and contacts the vendor directly.
  4. Vendor recovers some cost instead of a total loss.

For local pantries, food banks, and NGOs, not vendors

How a pantry or NGO joins the network.

This section is for the receiving side, local pantries, food banks, and NGOs who want to receive matched surplus, not for grocery vendors or restaurants listing food. Our seed pantry/NGO network in Kenya, Nigeria, and South Africa was built by hand for this prototype. Here's the process we're building toward as we onboard real partner pantries and NGOs.

1

Your pantry or NGO applies

Tell us where your pantry or NGO is located, and roughly how much surplus food your pantry or NGO can collect from vendors and store until it's distributed.

2

We verify your pantry or NGO

We confirm your pantry or NGO is a real, currently operating organization, and review its basic food handling and storage safety practices.

3

Your pantry or NGO sets up a profile

Your pantry or NGO's location, storage capacity, and contact number go into the matching engine, so nearby vendor listings can start being routed to you.

4

Your pantry or NGO starts receiving matches

Your pantry or NGO gets an automated pickup alert the moment a nearby vendor's surplus listing fits your capacity, first come, first served.

Questions

Common questions

How do you handle food safety?

Every listing includes a strict pickup window based on how long the food stays genuinely safe to eat, and vendors confirm food is unspoiled at the time of listing. As we onboard real partners, we're building a lightweight food safety check into the NGO verification step, so organizations we work with meet basic handling standards before they're added to the matching pool.

Is this free for vendors?

Yes, for donations. Listing surplus and getting matched to an NGO costs nothing. The "sell to the community" path lets a vendor set their own discounted price instead, for surplus that's a better fit for a small recovery than a full donation.

Is help limited to NGOs, or can individuals get free food too?

Neither is exclusive. The matching engine's automated alert goes to the best-fit local pantry or NGO first, but every donation listing also stays visible on our public live board. That means a vulnerable individual or family, not just a registered organization, can see an unclaimed donation and arrange pickup directly with the vendor, free of charge, the same way a pantry or NGO would.

Do vendors need any certification to use Baki?

No certification is required to list surplus food. We rely on the vendor's own confirmation that food is safe and unspoiled, the same standard most food donation programs use.

Which countries does Baki operate in?

Kenya, Nigeria, and South Africa today, live specifically in Nairobi, Lagos, and Johannesburg, the biggest commercial hubs in each country, chosen because that's where our team has direct local knowledge.

About Baki

The coordination layer food redistribution has been missing.

Local grocery vendors, along with restaurants and small hotels, dump surplus perishable food every day, not because no one wants it, but because there's no automated, hyper-local way to connect it to the local pantries and NGOs who could redistribute it. Baki is that coordination hub. A vendor logs a listing once; our matching engine scores nearby pantries and NGOs by distance, urgency, and available capacity, and fires an automated pickup alert within seconds. When a listing is a better fit for a discounted sale than a full donation, it goes straight to a public community board instead, giving the vendor a way to recover some value rather than a total loss.

We operate across Kenya, Nigeria, and South Africa, specifically Nairobi, Lagos, and Johannesburg, in markets where food waste is well documented at the household level but almost entirely unmeasured at the point where we work: retail and food service. That gap is both the problem and the opportunity.
