## CONCEPT NOTE
# BRAID (Equitable Mentorship Pairing Engine)
Project Name: BraidTeam: Quantum SolversUN Sustainable Development Goal: Goal 5 – Gender EqualityProject Outcome: A working mentorship platform
# Project Overview
Braid (EMPE) is a digital mentorship platform designed to improve access to structured, compatible, and equitable mentorship relationships. The project was developed in response to the challenge faced by early-career women in technical fields who may have limited access to professional networks, experienced mentors, and structured opportunities for career development.
Rather than relying solely on informal networking or manual mentor–mentee pairing, Braid provides a structured environment in which organisations can collect participant information, define matching requirements, generate compatible mentor–mentee matches, review matching outcomes, and support the management of mentorship programmes.
The platform places the matching engine at the centre of the solution. The matching process is designed to consider defined requirements, participant preferences, mentor capacity, and equity considerations rather than relying only on simple profile similarity. This allows programme coordinators to have greater visibility and control over how matches are generated and reviewed.
The project focuses on developing a working prototype of the core Braid solution, particularly the participant information, matching, administrative oversight, security, and validation components required to demonstrate the concept.
Background and Problem Statement
Early-career women in technical fields can experience difficulty accessing professional mentors, networks, role models, and structured opportunities that support career development. These challenges exist within a broader gender gap in STEM education, digital skills, and participation in technology-related careers.
According to UNESCO, women represent only 35% of STEM graduates globally, a figure that has remained largely unchanged over the past decade. Women also account for only 26% of the workforce in data and artificial intelligence. In Sub-Saharan Africa, digital-skills inequalities are particularly pronounced: UNESCO reports that for every 100 men with spreadsheet skills, only approximately 40–44 women possess the same level of proficiency. These disparities can limit women's ability to participate fully in an increasingly digital economy and to access technology-related professional opportunities.
The challenge extends beyond formal education and technical skills. The United Nations Office of the Special Adviser on Africa reported in 2026 that fewer than 25% of tertiary students in Africa pursue STEM, while in 17 of 21 countries with available sex-disaggregated data, women account for 16% or less of STEM graduates. The same analysis identifies limited exposure, insufficient role models, and weak mentorship networks as factors contributing to women's underrepresentation in STEM pathways. It further emphasizes mentorship as an important mechanism for connecting education to professional development and STEM careers.
Access to digital connectivity also influences women’s ability to benefit from online professional networks, learning opportunities, and mentorship. In Sub-Saharan Africa, the gender gap in mobile internet adoption stood at 26% in 2025, with approximately 230 million women in the region not using mobile internet (GSMA, 2026). More than two-thirds of the 810 million women who remain unconnected live in South Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa. This digital access gap is important for mentorship platforms because professional networking, learning, communication, and career-development opportunities increasingly depend on digital connectivity.
Evidence from the World Bank's Africa Gender Innovation Lab further demonstrates the importance of addressing structural barriers to women's economic empowerment. The Lab has conducted more than 100 impact evaluations across 30 African countries to identify scalable interventions that can improve women's economic opportunities and empowerment. This body of work reinforces the importance of evidence-based interventions that address barriers to women's participation in economic and professional life.
The African context also demonstrates the practical value of mentorship and professional networks. The United Nations Office of the Special Adviser on Africa identifies mentorship as an important pathway for helping young people, particularly girls and women, navigate STEM education and transition into professional careers. Existing initiatives across Africa have used mentorship, role models, and professional exposure to help women and girls develop technical skills, confidence, networks, and career aspirations.
Despite these efforts, access to mentorship remains uneven, and many mentorship programmes rely on informal networks or manual administrative processes for collecting participant information, pairing mentors and mentees, monitoring relationships, and evaluating programme outcomes. Informal networking can favour individuals who already have access to established professional communities, while manual matching becomes increasingly difficult as programmes grow.
The original project problem statement identifies the challenge as:
“Early-career women in technical fields face systemic isolation due to a lack of structured access to professional network matching.” This problem statement remains unchanged and is preserved as the core problem definition for the project.
Braid therefore responds to an evidence-supported challenge at the intersection of gender equality, STEM participation, digital inclusion, professional networking, and mentorship access. The platform is designed to improve the structure, transparency, scalability, and equity of mentorship matching processes within organisations, rather than attempting to solve the broader structural causes of gender inequality through technology alone.
### Proposed Solution
Braid (EMPE) provides a structured digital environment for running mentorship programmes.
At the organisational level, the platform allows an organisation or programme coordinator to manage mentorship programmes and participants. Participants can provide information about their experience, skills, goals, preferences, and other programme-specific information through structured forms.
Programme coordinators can then define the criteria that should influence matching. The matching engine uses these criteria to generate proposed mentor–mentee relationships. The process supports the generation of matching runs, review of proposed matches, consideration of fairness and capacity, and coordinator intervention where necessary.
Once matches are approved, participants can engage through structured mentorship relationships known as strands. A strand can support either a one-to-one or group mentorship arrangement. The platform can also support relationship activities such as sessions, goals, milestones, check-ins, and other forms of engagement tracking.
This approach moves Braid (EMPE) beyond simply finding a person who appears compatible. It seeks to provide an end-to-end structure for managing mentorship programmes from participant onboarding and matching through relationship monitoring and reporting.
### Core Objectives
The project aims to:
- Improve access to structured mentorship by providing a digital platform through which organisations can manage and connect participants within mentorship programmes.
- Develop an equitable matching process that considers defined compatibility requirements, participant preferences, mentor capacity, and fairness considerations.
- Reduce the administrative burden of mentorship programmes by supporting participant data collection, matching, programme management, relationship tracking, and reporting within one platform.
- Improve transparency in matching decisions by allowing programme coordinators to review proposed matches and understand the factors contributing to matching outcomes.
- Validate the effectiveness of the proposed matching approach through structured testing using representative mentor and mentee profiles and matching scenarios.

### Key Platform Capabilities
The final platform is structured around several interconnected capabilities.
#### Organisation and Programme Management
Organisations can manage mentorship programmes and their associated participants. This allows the platform to support multiple programmes rather than treating mentorship as a single isolated matching exercise.
#### Participant Profiles and Applications
Participants provide relevant information through structured application and profile processes. Programme-specific forms allow organisations to collect information appropriate to the type of mentorship programme being conducted.
#### Configurable Matching
Programme coordinators can define the criteria used for matching. The matching process can consider required conditions and weighted preferences rather than applying one fixed matching rule to every programme.
#### Equitable Matching
Equity is incorporated into the matching process by considering fairness-related rules, participant priorities, mentor capacity, and the distribution of available mentorship opportunities.
The intention is not simply to produce the highest compatibility score. The system should also help coordinators identify potential imbalances and make informed decisions before matches are finalised.
#### Matching Review and Overrides
Matching is treated as a reviewable process rather than an irreversible automated decision. Proposed matches can be reviewed by programme coordinators before they are published.
Where necessary, coordinators can make appropriate adjustments to proposed matches.
#### Mentorship Strands
After matching, participants are connected through mentorship strands. Strands can support one-to-one or group mentorship arrangements and provide a structure for managing the ongoing relationship.
#### Mentorship Engagement
The platform supports the tracking of mentorship activities such as sessions, goals, milestones, and check-ins. This allows the programme to monitor not only whether a match was made but also whether the mentorship relationship is progressing.
#### Monitoring and Reporting
Programme-level information can be used to monitor participation, matching outcomes, engagement, and other relevant programme indicators. This gives coordinators information that can support programme evaluation and future improvements.
### The Equitable Matching Approach
The matching engine is the central technical component of Braid (EMPE).
The final approach moves beyond a simple compatibility score. Matching can incorporate:
- Required or hard matching constraints
- Weighted participant preferences
- Skills and experience
- Goals and interests
- Mentor capacity
- Programme-defined priorities
- Equity and fairness considerations
- Availability of suitable mentors
- Coverage and unmatched participants
The matching process is designed to operate as a matching run. A coordinator can initiate a run, review the resulting proposed matches, examine relevant matching information, identify unmatched participants, make appropriate adjustments, and publish the final results.
This human-in-the-loop approach recognises that mentorship is a human relationship and that an algorithm should support programme coordinators rather than make every decision without oversight.
### Expected Benefits
The platform is expected to provide benefits at both participant and organisational levels.
For participants, Braid (EMPE) aims to:
- Improve access to mentors outside their immediate personal networks.
- Increase the likelihood of meaningful mentor–mentee compatibility.
- Provide structured support for professional development.
- Create clearer pathways for setting and monitoring mentorship goals.
- Support continued engagement after the initial match.
For organisations, Braid (EMPE) aims to:
- Reduce reliance on spreadsheets and manual matching.
- Make large mentorship programmes easier to administer.
- Provide greater visibility into matching decisions.
- Support more consistent and transparent matching processes.
- Monitor mentorship engagement and relationship health.
- Generate useful programme-level information for evaluation.
### Equity and Gender Equality
Braid (EMPE) aligns with UN Sustainable Development Goal 5: Gender Equality by addressing barriers that can limit women’s access to professional networks, mentorship, and career-development opportunities.
The platform’s equity focus is reflected not only in its target problem but also in the design of the matching process. By allowing programme-level rules and fairness considerations to influence matching, Braid (EMPE) seeks to make access to mentorship more structured and less dependent on informal networks.
The project therefore views technology as an enabler of access and coordination rather than as a complete solution to the broader structural challenges faced by women in technical fields.
### Project Scope
The project focuses on the development of a working mentorship platform capable of supporting the core lifecycle of a structured mentorship programme:
Programme creation → Participant onboarding → Information collection → Matching configuration → Matching run → Match review → Match publication → Mentorship engagement → Progress monitoring → Reporting
The project does not claim that technology alone can eliminate all barriers to women’s professional advancement. Instead, it focuses on improving the accessibility, structure, administration, and equity of mentorship programmes.
### Expected Project Outcome
The primary outcome of the project is a working prototype of Braid, an equitable mentorship pairing engine that demonstrates how organisations can manage structured mentorship programmes and use an equity-aware matching process to connect participants.
The platform is intended to demonstrate the practical application of technology to a social and professional development challenge while providing a foundation that can be expanded into a larger mentorship management solution.

### Conclusion
Braid (EMPE) responds to a practical challenge: connecting people to meaningful mentorship opportunities in a way that is structured, scalable, and more equitable.
While the original concept focused primarily on compatibility-based mentor discovery, the final solution has developed into a broader mentorship programme platform in which the matching engine forms the core decision-support component.
By combining structured participant information, configurable matching criteria, equity considerations, coordinator review, mentorship strands, engagement tracking, and reporting, Braid (EMPE) provides a foundation for organisations to manage mentorship programmes more effectively.
The project ultimately seeks to demonstrate that technology can reduce the barriers associated with discovering, matching, and managing mentorship relationships while supporting more equitable access to professional development opportunities.
