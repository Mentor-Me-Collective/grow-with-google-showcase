## Project Purpose
The purpose of Braid is to develop a working digital platform that improves how structured mentorship programmes are organised, managed, and matched.
The platform addresses the challenge of limited structured access to professional mentorship, particularly for early-career women in technical fields. Instead of depending entirely on informal professional networks or manual mentor–mentee pairing, Braid provides a structured environment for organisations to manage mentorship programmes and connect participants through an equity-aware matching process.
The project places the matching engine at the centre of the solution. However, the platform extends beyond matching by supporting participant onboarding, programme management, mentorship relationships, engagement tracking, and reporting.
## Background
Early-career women in technical fields can face barriers to accessing experienced professionals, career guidance, and networks that support professional growth.
Mentorship programmes can help address these barriers, but their administration can become difficult when participant numbers increase. Collecting participant information, determining compatibility, assigning mentors, monitoring relationships, and evaluating outcomes can require significant manual effort.
Braid (EMPE) was conceived to address this operational gap by providing a structured digital system through which organisations can run mentorship programmes.
The project is aligned with SDG 5 which talks about Gender Equality because it focuses on improving access to professional development and mentorship opportunities that can contribute to women’s participation and advancement in technical fields.
## Problem Statement
Early-career women in technical fields may lack structured access to mentors with relevant experience, skills, and career knowledge. Where mentorship programmes exist, matching may depend on manual processes or existing professional networks, which can make the process difficult to scale and may limit equitable access to suitable mentors.
There is therefore a need for a platform that can support structured participant onboarding, configurable matching, equitable allocation of mentorship opportunities, and continued monitoring of mentorship relationships.
## Project Goal
To develop a working mentorship platform that enables organisations to create and manage structured mentorship programmes and use an equitable, configurable matching process to connect participants with suitable mentors.
## Project Objectives
The project objectives are to:
- Develop a working digital mentorship platform for managing structured mentorship programmes by 14th August 2026.
- Implement a configurable matching process by 7th August 2026 that considers participant information, compatibility requirements, preferences, mentor capacity, and equity considerations.
- Reduce the administrative effort involved in participant management and mentor–mentee matching by at least 50%.
- Provide functional tools for managing mentorship relationships by 14th August 2026 after matching through strands, sessions, goals, milestones, and check-ins.
- Support greater transparency and oversight of matching decisions by 14th August 2026 through coordinator review and matching outcome information.
## Project Scope
### In Scope
The project includes the development and demonstration of the following capabilities:
#### Organisation and Programme Management
- Creation and management of mentorship programmes.
- Programme-specific participant management.
- Programme configuration and administration.
- Management of programme participation.
#### Participant Management
- Participant registration and onboarding.
- Participant profiles.
- Collection of programme-specific participant information.
- Participant directory and roster management.
#### Matching
- Definition of matching criteria.
- Hard matching constraints.
- Weighted matching preferences.
- Mentor capacity considerations.
- Equity and fairness considerations.
- Matching runs.
- Proposed/draft matches.
- Match review.
- Coordinator overrides.
- Unmatched participant handling.
- Final match publication.
#### Mentorship Management
- Creation of mentorship strands.
- Support for one-to-one and group mentorship.
- Session tracking.
- Goal tracking.
- Milestone tracking.
- Check-ins and relationship monitoring.
#### Monitoring and Reporting
- Programme-level monitoring.
- Matching outcome information.
- Engagement information.
- Reporting and evaluation support.
- Relevant activity/audit information.
### Out of Scope
The following are outside the core scope of the project unless explicitly implemented as part of the final working platform:
- Providing professional mentorship directly.
- Guaranteeing career advancement or employment outcomes.
- Replacing human judgement in all matching decisions.
- Eliminating organisational or societal barriers affecting women’s career advancement.
- Providing unrestricted social networking functionality unrelated to mentorship.
- Guaranteeing long-term programme outcomes beyond the project period.

## Success Criteria
The project will be considered successful when:
- A working version of the Braid (EMPE) platform can be demonstrated.
- Users can be onboarded into a mentorship programme.
- Relevant participant information can be collected and managed.
- A programme coordinator can configure and execute a matching process.
- The matching process can produce proposed mentorship matches using defined criteria.
- Matching outcomes can be reviewed before finalisation.
- Participants can be connected through mentorship strands.
- Core mentorship engagement information can be recorded or monitored.
- The platform demonstrates consideration of equity and fairness within the matching process.
- The final implementation and supporting documentation are available in the project repository.
## Key Risks
| Risk | Potential Impact | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Matching algorithm produces unsuitable matches | Reduced user satisfaction and confidence | Test matching logic using representative scenarios and allow coordinator review/override |
| Insufficient participant data | Reduced matching quality | Define required fields and validate participant information |
| Unmatched participants | Some participants may not receive suitable mentors | Provide an unmatched queue and allow programme-level review |
| Limited development time | Incomplete functionality | Prioritise core MVP functionality and document future enhancements |
| Security or privacy issues | Exposure of sensitive participant information | Apply appropriate authentication, access control, privacy, and security testing |
| Changing requirements | Rework and documentation inconsistency | Maintain a clear scope and document approved changes |
| Low user engagement | Mentorship relationships may become inactive | Include sessions, goals, milestones, and check-ins to support engagement |
## Assumptions
The project is based on the following assumptions:
- Mentorship programmes will provide relevant participant information for matching.
- Programme coordinators will define or approve matching criteria.
- Mentors will provide accurate information regarding their skills, experience, preferences, and capacity.
- Human review remains valuable when finalising algorithm-generated matches.
- Users will have access to the platform and the information required to participate.
- The working platform represents a prototype outcome and may require further development before production-scale deployment.

The project is constrained by:

## Change Management
The project evolved from an initial compatibility-focused mentorship discovery concept into a broader structured mentorship platform.
The major design change was the development of a more configurable and equity-aware matching approach. As a result, the final project documentation treats the matching engine not simply as a recommendation feature but as a core programme-management capability involving criteria, matching runs, review, fairness considerations, capacity, and human oversight.
Future changes to the project should be evaluated against:
- Project objectives.
- Available development time.
- Technical feasibility.
- User value.
- Equity implications.
- Impact on existing functionality.
- Impact on project documentation and deliverables.

### Summary
Braid (EMPE) is a working mentorship platform designed to help organisations manage structured mentorship programmes and connect participants through an equitable, configurable matching process. The project combines participant management, programme administration, matching, mentorship relationship tracking, and monitoring to create a more structured approach to mentorship access and delivery.
The matching engine is the central differentiating component of the solution, but the platform provides the surrounding programme infrastructure necessary for organisations to turn matching outcomes into sustained mentorship relationships.
